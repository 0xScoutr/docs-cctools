How rewards are allocated varies by campaign, but CCTools prioritizes verifiable on-chain proof over self-reporting. Every campaign publishes its rules before it opens, so you always know the criteria before you act.

<Note>
  CCTools verifies task completion using on-chain data where possible. Social tasks are verified via connected social accounts — make sure your accounts are linked in **Settings → Profile → Connections** before completing social tasks.
</Note>

## Verification methods

Different campaign types use different verification approaches. The campaign detail page always explains which method applies.

**On-chain activity tracking**

For bridging, swapping, liquidity provision, and other on-chain actions, CCTools monitors your wallet address directly on Canton Network. When you complete a qualifying transaction, it is read from the chain and matched against campaign criteria — no manual submission needed. Your wallet address is the proof.

Examples of what gets tracked:
- Bridge transaction hash and amount
- Swap volume over the campaign window
- Liquidity position size and duration
- Token balance at a snapshot block

**Leaderboard ranking**

Some campaigns rank participants rather than awarding a flat reward. Temple's market maker campaign, for example, sorts participants by verified on-chain settlement volume. Your position on the leaderboard at campaign close determines your reward tier. Leaderboards are typically visible in real time so you can gauge your standing.

**Task completion verification**

For off-chain tasks such as social sharing or upvoting, CCTools verifies completion through your connected social accounts. When you click **Verify** on a task, CCTools checks the connected account for the required action (a post, a follow, an upvote). Tasks that cannot be verified will not count toward your reward.

## Fair distribution

CCTools and partner projects are committed to transparent, rule-based reward distribution. Campaign rules — including eligibility criteria, reward amounts, and distribution timelines — are published on the campaign page before the campaign opens. Rule changes after a campaign starts are not permitted.

Reward pools are fixed at the start of each campaign. If a campaign awards rewards proportionally (for example, by bridge volume), the per-user amount depends on total participation.

## When rewards are distributed

Rewards are typically distributed within a few days of campaign close. The exact timeline is stated on the campaign page. Rewards may be:

- **Sent to your wallet** — CC and project tokens are sent directly to the wallet address that completed the on-chain tasks
- **Credited as XP** — XP is applied to your CCTools profile automatically
- **Distributed by the project team** — some project token distributions are handled directly by the partner; the campaign page will note if this is the case

If you don't see your reward immediately after the campaign closes, wait 24 hours. Canton Network indexers can introduce short delays in reward processing.

## Disputes

If you believe you completed all required tasks but did not receive a reward, follow these steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Wait 24 hours after campaign close">
    Indexer delays are the most common cause of missing rewards. Most cases resolve on their own within 24 hours.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check your wallet address">
    Confirm that the wallet address shown in your CCTools profile is the one you used for on-chain tasks. Rewards are sent to the completing wallet, not your profile email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contact the campaign partner">
    Each campaign is run in partnership with a specific project. Reach out to that project's team via their Discord or the CCTools [feedback form]("https://cctools.network/feedback") and include your wallet address, the campaign name, and a description of the tasks you completed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  CCTools can verify on-chain activity but cannot override reward decisions made by partner project smart contracts. Always check the campaign rules before participating to understand how edge cases are handled.
</Warning>
