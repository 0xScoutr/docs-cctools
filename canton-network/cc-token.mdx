Canton Coin (CC) is the native cryptocurrency of Canton Network. It powers the network's economic layer: you pay CC as transaction fees when you use Canton apps, validators earn CC as rewards for running network infrastructure, and CC is the unit of value exchanged across Canton's DEXes, wallets, and institutional platforms. Understanding how CC works — its supply mechanics, halving schedule, and where to get it — is fundamental to participating in the Canton ecosystem.

## Earning CC

The primary way CC enters circulation is through validator rewards. Validators and Super Validators earn CC each round for securing the network and participating in governance. The amount earned depends on the validator's tier, the current issuance rate per round, and the number of halvings that have already occurred.

Beyond running a validator, there are other ways to accumulate CC:

- **Staking delegation** — some validators allow you to delegate stake to them and share in a portion of their CC rewards
- **Ecosystem app campaigns** — Canton projects regularly run campaigns on CCTools where you can earn CC or other rewards by completing tasks, providing liquidity, or engaging with their platform
- **DEX trading rewards** — select Canton DEXes offer liquidity provider rewards denominated in CC

If you want to model potential CC returns before committing capital, use the [Rewards Calculator](/canton-network/rewards-calculator) to estimate earnings across all three participation types.

## CC halving

Like Bitcoin, Canton Coin has a **halving mechanism** built into its issuance schedule. Validator minting rewards are reduced at predetermined intervals, progressively lowering the rate at which new CC enters circulation. This deflationary mechanic means:

- Early validators and participants earn at higher issuance rates
- As the network matures and usage grows, transaction fee burns can outpace minting — pushing the burn/mint ratio above 1.0 and making CC net-deflationary
- The total supply of CC is bounded over the long run

CCTools tracks the halving countdown live on the [Network Stats](/network/network-stats) page, alongside the current burn/mint ratio updated hourly. You can see in real time whether the network is currently net-inflationary or net-deflationary based on actual on-chain activity.

<Note>
  The Network Stats page shows the current issuance rates per round for Super Validators, Validators, and App coupons — useful context when reading the halving countdown and burn/mint ratio together.
</Note>

## Getting CC

You can acquire CC through several venues, each with different fee structures, KYC requirements, and supported regions. The main options are:

| Venue | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cantex** | 0.05% | Canton-native DEX, no KYC for basic trading |
| **Temple** | 0.10% | Institutional-grade interface, may require KYC |
| **OneSwap** | 0.30% | Standard AMM model |
| **Bron** | 0.00% | Zero-fee wallet and exchange app |

The [Markets → Get CC](https://cctools.network/markets/get-cc) page on CCTools compares all available venues side by side — showing current price, 24-hour volume, KYC requirements, fees, and regional availability. Use it to find the best venue for your situation before you trade.

Bridging from other networks (Base, Ethereum, Solana) is also coming soon, which will allow you to bring external assets into Canton and swap them for CC without needing a Canton-native account first.

<Tip>
  Before buying CC, check the Markets → Get CC page to compare venues. You can filter by KYC requirement, fee level, and your country to find the most suitable option — especially useful if you're in a region where certain exchanges have restrictions.
</Tip>
