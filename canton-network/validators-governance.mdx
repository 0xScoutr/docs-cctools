Canton Network is secured and governed by a network of validators — institutional and independent operators who run the infrastructure that keeps the chain running. Unlike many blockchain networks where validators are anonymous stakers, Canton's validator set is largely composed of known, regulated entities, which gives the network a verifiable layer of accountability. CCTools gives you a live view of all validator activity and on-chain governance, so you can follow the decisions shaping the network in real time.

## Validator roles

Canton Network has two tiers of validators, each with different responsibilities and levels of governance participation:

**Regular Validators** run Canton Network nodes and earn CC rewards for doing so. Their primary role is infrastructure: processing transactions, maintaining uptime, and contributing to the network's overall security. They are eligible for per-round CC rewards based on their tier and performance.

**Super Validators** do everything regular validators do, plus they have additional governance responsibilities that give them a direct role in shaping how the network operates:

- **Price consensus rounds** — Super Validators participate in regular rounds to establish a consensus on the CC/USD reference price, which is used across the network for fee calculations and reward distributions
- **Network configuration changes** — Super Validators vote on changes to Canton Network parameters, such as issuance rates and protocol upgrades
- **On-chain proposal voting** — governance proposals require Super Validator approval to pass; each Super Validator's vote is recorded on-chain and visible to anyone

Being a Super Validator is not just a technical role — it is a governance commitment. Super Validators are accountable for the decisions they make on-chain, and CCTools surfaces their voting records so you can evaluate their participation.

## Global Synchronizer Foundation (GSF)

The **Global Synchronizer Foundation (GSF)** is the formal governance body overseeing Canton Network. The GSF coordinates the validator set, sets the framework for on-chain governance, and provides the institutional structure within which proposals are debated and executed.

The GSF's validator membership includes some of the world's most prominent financial institutions — among them Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, and BNY Mellon. This institutional backing is a core part of Canton Network's value proposition: the chain is governed by entities with deep regulatory expertise and reputational skin in the game, not anonymous token holders.

For CCTools users, the GSF's role means that governance on Canton is more structured than on typical public blockchains. Proposals go through a defined process, Super Validators vote with their identities on record, and the outcomes are enforced through the protocol automatically.

## Monitoring governance on CCTools

The [Governance](https://cctools.network/governance) page on CCTools is your window into Canton Network's on-chain decision-making. Everything is sourced directly from the Canton Network and updated in real time.

On the Governance page you can see:

- **Active proposals** — proposals currently open for Super Validator voting, including proposal type, description, and current vote tally
- **Historical proposals** — the full archive of past governance actions, with final vote counts, approval or rejection status, and execution records
- **Super Validator vote records** — for each proposal, you can see exactly how each Super Validator voted, giving you a detailed view of individual participation rates and positions
- **Price consensus rounds** — a log of completed price consensus rounds, showing the reference price established each round and which Super Validators participated
- **Network configuration changes** — a record of approved parameter changes, such as issuance rate adjustments, that have been applied to the network
