## API keys

Every request to a `/api/v1/public/` endpoint authenticates with a key in the **`X-API-Key`** header, except
requests to `/api/v1/public/status`, which needs none. The pay-per-call route under `/api/v1/x402/` needs no key (see [Pay per call with x402](/api/x402)):

```bash
curl -H "X-API-Key: cck_your_key_here" \
  "https://api.cctools.network/api/v1/public/network/stats"
```

Keys are strings of the form `cck_` followed by 56 hex characters. There is no
`Authorization: Bearer` support. From a browser a Bearer token returns `401`.
From a command-line client such as curl or python-requests it is rejected one
layer earlier, as `403 {"error":"Forbidden"}` with no `code` field, because a
request carrying no `X-API-Key` is not recognised as an API client at all.
Always send `X-API-Key`.

Create, list, and revoke keys at
[cctools.network/profile/api](https://cctools.network/profile/api). The
cleartext is shown exactly once at creation; afterward only the prefix is
visible. Once you hold one,
[cctools.network/api-docs](https://cctools.network/api-docs) runs any endpoint
against it in the browser.

How many active keys you can hold depends on your plan; the number for each is
in [Plans and limits](/api/plans). The per-minute limit is counted per key and
endpoint, so each extra key adds throughput, while the daily limit is counted
for the whole account and does not grow with the number of keys.

## Plans

Your key has the plan of your account, and the plan sets your rate limits. It is
echoed on every response as `meta.tier`.

| Plan | Rate limit | Who can have it |
|---|---|---|
| **Trial or Evaluation** | 60 requests per minute, 1,000 calls per day | The trial, open once per account to accounts with a connected wallet that pass the CCTools human check, or an evaluation key from our team. For testing only. |
| **Pro** | 60 requests per minute, 1,000 calls per day | Included with a [CCTools Pro](https://cctools.network/pro) subscription, for personal use. Granted instantly, and runs for as long as the subscription does. |
| **Data Starter** | 120 requests per minute, 20,000 calls per day | Bought in Canton Coin, monthly or annual. Commercial use, with attribution. |
| **Data Team** | 600 requests per minute, no daily cap | Bought in Canton Coin, monthly or annual. Commercial use, with attribution. |
| **Validator** | No per-key limit, no daily cap | A Canton validator party ID. Every submission is reviewed by a person, usually within one business day. 12-month term, free. |
| **Enterprise** | No per-key limit, no daily cap | Commercial use inside your product, under an order we agree. Contact us. |

<Warning>
No per-key limit is not the same as no limit. Every request to `/api/`, on
every plan including Validator, also counts against a ceiling of **600
requests per minute per IP address**. Sustained breaches escalate to a
temporary block on the IP, so back off on a `429` rather than retrying at
once. Planning a backfill that needs more than that? Ask us first.
</Warning>

The limits count differently. The per-minute limit is per key and endpoint. The
daily limit is per account, over a UTC day, and the calls of all your own keys add up.

**What each plan may be used for.** The trial is for testing and Pro is for
personal use. The Data plans allow commercial use, with attribution, of the
endpoints marked **Data plans** in the Use column of the
[endpoint reference](/api/endpoints). The endpoints marked **Personal use** are
personal use on every plan. The terms are at
[cctools.network/terms/api](https://cctools.network/terms/api), and the figures
and prices at [Plans and limits](/api/plans).

The Free plan has ended. A key made on it answers `401` with the code
`tier_retired` and a `renew_url` that points to the plans: take a plan, then
create a new key. A Pro period you paid for before the plans changed keeps 600
requests per minute and no daily cap until that period ends.

## Getting a plan

Open [/profile/api](https://cctools.network/profile/api). The plan panel shows
what your account can take, and **New key** stays off until it holds a plan.

- **Pro**: with a [CCTools Pro](https://cctools.network/pro) subscription, the Pro card grants Pro instantly (**Unlock Pro access**).
- **Trial**: **Start the trial** is open once per account to accounts with a connected wallet that pass the CCTools human check. It is for testing only, and it draws on a daily budget shared by all trial accounts.
- **Data Starter and Data Team**: **Buy Data Starter** or **Buy Data Team** and pay in Canton Coin, for a month or a year. You accept the [API terms](https://cctools.network/terms/api) at checkout. A plan does not renew by itself: access ends at the end of the period unless you renew. A larger plan starts at once and the rest of a smaller one resumes after it; a smaller plan bought during a larger one waits its turn.
- **Validator**: submit a party ID on the same page.
- **Enterprise**: [contact us](https://cctools.network/contact).

Building something that needs more than this? Apply on the same page and tell us
what you are building. Higher and custom limits exist; they are arranged per
account rather than picked from a list.

**The trial's check runs when you start it, never on the requests you make with
it.** Keys we issue directly to an organisation are attached to that
organisation rather than to a person, so there is no account to check and
nothing for a server-to-server integration to keep alive. A key stops working
for these reasons: it is revoked, it expires or the plan behind it ends (`401`
with the code `plan_ended`), or it is called outside its scopes.

**Your keys follow your account's plan.** A key you make takes the limits of the
plan your account holds, and it runs until that plan's coverage ends. When you
renew Pro, buy a Data plan or start the trial, your existing keys take the new
coverage, so there is no new key to make: after renewing Pro, press **Extend access**
on [/profile/api](https://cctools.network/profile/api) and your keys are
extended to match. Pressing it never lowers anything: a Validator or Enterprise
plan granted to your account stays. When no plan is left, your keys answer `401`
with the code `plan_ended` and a `renew_url`, and renewing brings them back.

## Rate limits

Two limits count your calls.

The **per-minute limit** is counted per key, per endpoint, per minute, **per process**. The API runs as a
multi-process cluster where each process keeps its own counter, so a client
spreading requests across many connections can exceed the stated figure before
anything pushes back. Treat the number as the sustained rate to design for
rather than a ceiling to probe.

When you do exceed it you get `429` with a `Retry-After: 60` header:

```json
{ "error": "Rate limit exceeded", "code": "rate_limited" }
```

The **daily limit** is counted per account over a UTC day, from 00:00 to 24:00
UTC. The calls of all your own keys add up (a key issued by our team has a cap
of its own), and a call that a limit refuses is not counted. Over the cap you
get `429` with the code `daily_limit`, a `Retry-After` set to the seconds left
until 00:00 UTC, and `X-RateLimit-Remaining-Day: 0`:

```json
{ "error": "Rate limit exceeded", "code": "daily_limit" }
```

A key under a daily cap gets `X-RateLimit-Limit-Day` and
`X-RateLimit-Remaining-Day` on every successful response and on the
`daily_limit` `429` (the per-minute `429` does not carry them). The trial also
draws on a budget shared by all trial accounts: when it is spent, the answer is
the same `429` `daily_limit`, until 00:00 UTC.

Every successful response also carries your ceiling in
`meta.rate_limit_remaining`. On plans with no per-key limit it is `null`, so
type it as nullable. `meta.daily_limit_remaining` is the calls you have left
today; it is `null` when your plan has no daily cap or the counter could not be
read.

<Warning>
Despite the name, `rate_limit_remaining` is your **ceiling**, not a countdown.
It returns the same number on every response and never decrements, because an
exact per-request count would cost a database read on the hot path. Do not
branch on it dropping toward zero, since it never will. Use the `429` and its
`Retry-After` header as your real backoff signal. `daily_limit_remaining` is the
exception: it does count down.
</Warning>

## Errors

| Status | `code` | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `400` | endpoint-specific | Malformed parameter, e.g. `invalid_slug`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_party_id`. |
| `401` | `unauthorized` | Missing, malformed, revoked, or expired key. |
| `401` | `plan_ended` | The plan behind the key has ended. The `renew_url` says where to renew; renewing brings the key back. |
| `401` | `tier_retired` | The key was made on the Free plan, which has ended. The `renew_url` points to the plans. |
| `403` | `forbidden` | The resource exists but is not public, e.g. a private profile. |
| `404` | endpoint-specific | No such resource, e.g. `project_not_found`, `post_not_found`. |
| `422` | `unsupported_token` | The project has no token to report on. |
| `429` | `rate_limited` | Over your per-minute limit. Retry after the header. |
| `429` | `daily_limit` | Over your daily cap, or the trial budget is spent. Retry after 00:00 UTC. |
| `500` | `internal` / `query_failed` | Our fault. Safe to retry. |
| `502` | `upstream_unavailable` | A market data source we depend on is down. |
| `503` | `warming_up` | A cron-warmed dataset has a cold cache. Retry in a minute; this is not an empty result. |

Most errors carry `{ "error": "...", "code": "..." }`, but the `code` is
specific to the endpoint rather than drawn from a fixed list. **Branch on the
HTTP status, which is stable, and treat `code` as detail.** Two responses come
from the edge layer before the handler runs and carry only `{ "error" }` with
no `code` at all: the `403` for a keyless tool client, and the `429` for the
per-IP ceiling.

## Good practice

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Keep keys server-side" icon="server">
    Never ship a key in a browser bundle or mobile app. Proxy through a backend
    you control. A leaked key can be used up to its rate limit until you revoke it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rotate on suspicion" icon="rotate">
    Revoking is immediate: the key starts returning `401` right away. Mint a
    replacement first, cut over, then revoke the old one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Handle 429 gracefully" icon="clock">
    Respect `Retry-After`. For steady workloads, stay under your plan's limits
    rather than bursting and backing off.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
